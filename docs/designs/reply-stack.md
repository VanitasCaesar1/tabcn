# Reply Stack

-   **Status:** Implemented (2026-11-25) from a screenshot and two screen recordings of X's new Android navigation bar that the owner shared. Measured against them and checked on the iOS simulator. Minimize on scroll, the docked reply row and the lens that follows the finger added 2026-11-27 from a third clip ([VerdeSelvans on X](https://x.com/VerdeSelvans/status/2125803556670509432), filmed off a phone's screen with a camera), then corrected after an independent review of the code and a second pass over the clip.
-   **Design:** `reply-stack`, a tab bar in `registry/`. One foundation fix came with it: a minimized floating bar lets touches through where it draws nothing (below, Touch).

## Overview

A frosted-glass bar for content apps. One wide glass capsule holds five icon-only tabs. A round glass lens glides behind the active tab, whose icon turns from an outline to a solid shape. On a post's page a second capsule of the same width sits above the first and holds the reply field: the viewer's photo, "Post your reply", and photo, GIF and expand buttons. The feed shows through both, blurred.

## The reference, measured

From the dark screenshot (1216 px wide) and the recordings (1080 px wide, light and dark pages), scaled to a 402 pt screen:

-   **Capsules.** Both are the screen's width less 18 pt a side. The tab capsule is 72 pt tall, the reply capsule 64 pt, 10 pt apart. The tab capsule's bottom edge is 20 pt above the screen's: lower than a usual floating bar, just clear of the home indicator.
-   **Tabs.** Five equal cells, 4 pt in from the capsule's ends. Glyphs are about 23 pt with a 2.4 pt stroke. Home, bell and chat turn solid when active.
-   **Lens.** It fills the active tab's cell, 4 pt inside the capsule's top and bottom (about 71 by 64 pt). Light: a grey glass bead, lighter at the top left, darker to the bottom right, with a white rim and a soft shadow. Dark: a translucent lighter disc with almost no rim.
-   **Glass.** Light: white frost (the page behind shows through at about 40%), a bright white edge and a soft grey shadow. Dark: a darkened blur with a faint edge that is brightest along the top right and the bottom left.
-   **Reply row.** The photo is 38 pt across, 10 pt in. "Post your reply" is about 17.5 pt in X's grey. The three action glyphs are about 21 pt, 56 pt apart, the last 37 pt from the capsule's end.
-   **Not in the first clips:** a tab change, or the bar minimizing. The third clip has both (next section).
-   **Not part of this design:** the round black compose button that floats above the bar's end in the recordings. It is a separate control; Companion Chip draws one.

The glyphs are drawn by hand to those proportions, not traced. The explore glyph is a plain ring with a blade through it, not xAI's Grok mark.

## The third clip, frame by frame

An 18.8 s clip at 30 fps, a camera filming a phone (a budget phone: a plainer blur than X's frost, the poster says). Every motion segment was found with a frame-difference pass and stepped through at 30 fps with large crops; sizes are measured against the pill, whose width is 366 pt (about 1.63 px per pt in the clip).

-   **Minimize on scroll (0.73 s, 1.93 s, 10.50 s; back at 3.07 s and 11.00 s).** Scrolling the feed down shrinks the bar to a glass circle at its start that holds only the active tab's icon, solid and at its full size, with no lens inside. The circle is about 52 pt across (0.72 of the pill's height), its bottom edge on the pill's. Shrinking takes 3 to 4 frames: the pill's end runs to its start while the other icons slide into the active one, overlapping as they fade. Growing back takes 4 to 5 frames, the icons sliding out of the active one.
-   **The reply row docks.** On a post's page the reply row drops into the circle's row, its end where it was, its glass about 10 pt from the circle. It shrinks to the circle's height and lines up with its middle (10.70 s, 14.0 s), and the photo shrinks to about half that height. Its actions don't move. The placeholder stays whole while the row moves and is cut to "Post your re…" as it lands (10.63 s); on the way back it is whole again as soon as the row leaves (11.03 s).
-   **The lens follows the finger (6.6 to 7.7 s).** With Alerts active, a finger slides left along the bar to Home. A grey lens runs with it over Explore and Search to Home while Alerts stays solid. The icons swap in one frame (8.13 to 8.17 s), about 0.4 s after the finger lifts, with the page change.
-   **The compose button (0.5 s, 3.4 s, 16.2 s).** A white disc with a dark plus, about 58 pt across (0.8 of the pill's height), its end on the pill's end and its bottom edge about 17 pt above the pill. As the bar minimizes it drops into the circle's row and shrinks to the circle's size, on the same frames. It isn't there on a post's page, where the reply row is.
-   **Timing, measured frame by frame (30 fps, so each figure is ±1 frame).** Shrinking: 0.73 to 0.83 s, 1.93 to 2.03 s and 10.50 to 10.60 s, so about 4 frames (130 ms), starting and ending gently. Growing: 3.07 to 3.20 s and 11.00 to 11.13 s, so about 5 to 6 frames (170 to 200 ms), fast at first and settling.

## Decisions

-   **One design, one bar.** `ReplyStack` draws both capsules inside `TabBarRoot placement="floating"`, so the bar box grows upward when the row appears and screens that pad with `useBottomTabBarHeight()` follow. The tab capsule doesn't move.
-   **The row belongs to the screen, not the tab.** A post's page is rarely a tab, so the row is opt-in: pass `reply` (placeholder, photo, `onPress`, `actions`) and drive `showReply` from your own state. Without `reply` there is no row. The demo shows it on Home only.
-   **Liquid Glass on iOS 26, frosted blur elsewhere** (2026-11-27, [`glass-material.md`](glass-material.md)). X's bar is a blur with a tint and a lit edge, and that blur is what `material="solid"` and systems without glass draw. With glass (the default `'auto'` on iOS 26) both capsules are glass views laid out on the same frame the blur's clip follows, so the minimize changes their size through layout, never a scale; the reply row's glass comes in and out with `glassEffectStyle` (`'none'` to `'regular'`, animated) while its content fades, and its grow and press dip move to the content. Before this change the design used `BlurView` on iOS and the web even where Liquid Glass was available. expo-blur's web blur is a fifth of the intensity in px, half as strong as iOS at the same value, so the web gets twice the intensity. Android can only blur what a `BlurTargetView` wraps, and a tab bar can't wrap the screens, so there it is a denser fill with the same edge.
-   **Files.** `reply-stack.tsx` (the bar: layout, minimize, touch), `reply-stack-tabs.tsx` (the lens and each tab), `reply-stack-row.tsx` (the reply row), `reply-stack-glass.tsx` (the glass, its shrinking frame, and the lens's bead), `reply-stack-icons.tsx`, all in the one registry item so each stays under about 450 lines ([composer-dock-split.md](composer-dock-split.md)).
-   **The edge and the lens are drawn once in SVG** at the capsule's size: a hairline outline, then a 1.2 pt rim whose gradient runs from the top right to the bottom left. react-native-svg drops the alpha of an `rgba()` stop colour, so colours are split into `stopColor` and `stopOpacity`.
-   **Light or dark.** `tint` picks the glass; without it the bar follows the system's appearance, as X does. Every colour can be overridden through `colors`.
-   **Motion (lively).** The lens answers the finger: it sets off for a tab as the finger lands on it, and follows the finger as it slides along the bar (`TabScrub` from `react-native-tabcn/gestures`, so the design needs react-native-gesture-handler). Lifting on a tab changes to it; lifting away from the bar, or a touch that doesn't end in a tab change, sends the lens back to the active tab (after 300 ms, time for the change to arrive). The active icon stays solid until the tab actually changes, as in the clip. The lens glides on `tabcnSpring` and stretches as it sets off: up to 14% wider and 5% flatter in 110 ms, springing back while the glide finishes, so it lands round. The icon swaps for its solid form over the middle of the focus change (progress 0.35 to 0.6 on `tabcnTiming`, about 25 ms), the one-frame swap of the clip. A pressed tab dips to 88% as the finger lands (`TabButton`'s press scale); a pressed reply field dips the whole row to 98% in 100 ms and springs back. The row rises 12 pt and grows from 96% over 220 ms (ease out), and sinks away in 150 ms (ease in: nobody tapped it away), about 70% of the entrance. Reduce motion makes every change instant.
-   **Fading glass.** iOS can't blur inside a view whose opacity, or any parent's, is below 1: the screen shows through sharp. So nothing above the row's blur fades. The blur's strength rises with the row's presence, and the tint, edge, content and shadow fade on their own; the row's content sits beside the glass, not inside it, and fades with it. The row stays mounted until its exit finishes, then unmounts so the bar's box shrinks back.
-   **Minimize on scroll.** On by default (`minimizeOnScroll`), through the foundation's `useMinimizeOnScroll`, `useTabBarMinimized` and `useRestoreTabBar`, as Split Search does. The pill shrinks to a 52 pt circle at its bottom start corner (the left, or the right in RTL); the icons keep their size. Icons gather into the active one as they fade, the kept icon slides to the circle's middle, and the lens fades: the circle is the glass. Past half-way, the circle is one button ("Home, show all tabs") that restores the bar without changing tab; the tabs behind it take no touches and are hidden from screen readers (`aria-hidden`) and, on the web, from the keyboard (`focusable`). The reply row drops 82 pt into the tabs' row, narrows from its start edge to sit 10 pt from the circle, and shrinks from 64 to 52 pt tall about its bottom edge, so it matches the circle and lines up with its middle; its content moves down 6 pt to stay centred, the photo shrinks from 38 to 26 pt, the placeholder rides in and the actions stay. (Before 2026-11-27 it kept its 64 pt beside the 52 pt circle, which made the circle look small and low.) The placeholder is laid out twice, at the full row's width and at the docked row's (ending in an ellipsis, no shrinking); the full one shows until progress 0.75 and the docked one takes over as the row lands, so on the way back the full text returns at once. The foundation's progress (on `tabcnTiming`, 200 ms either way) says when and which way; Reply Stack follows it on X's own timing instead: 130 ms to shrink (ease in and out) and 200 ms to grow (ease out), each started when the direction changes. The foundation's timing stays as it is for every other bar. The bar box keeps its height, so screens' padding doesn't move; a compose button placed with Companion Chip stays where it is.
-   **Shrinking glass.** One rounded clip shrinks over one blur that keeps its size and place (`Glass` and `useCapsuleFrame` in `reply-stack-glass.tsx`): the clip's left, top, width, height and radius animate, while the blur inside is held still by the opposite move, so it is never resized. The tabs sit in a second clip on the same frame. The edge is redrawn on the same frame each step (the SVG outline and rim's x, y, width, height and radius). An earlier version split the glass into two pieces, a still one and a sliding one, as Split Search does; with this translucent glass that showed a seam, because each blur also blurred the other's dark tint where they met, and each piece's shadow showed through the glass in a different place. One clip has no seam; the cost is a layout of a few plain views per frame for about 200 ms. On the web, react-native-svg's animated shapes drop the last frames of a change and stop short, so there the edge follows the progress through React state.
-   **Compose button.** Opt-in (`compose`: `onPress`, `label`, `icon`), as in X: a white disc with a dark plus on dark glass, a dark disc with a white plus on light. It floats 17 pt above the pill's end; minimizing, it drops into the circle's row and shrinks to 52 pt on the bar's own motion. It gives way to the reply row (fades, takes no touches, hidden from screen readers) because X shows one or the other. It sits outside the bar box, so it takes touches and screen readers find it ("Post"), and a screen's padding doesn't count it. A pressed button dips to 90% and springs back, with a selection tick.
-   **Touch.** The field and each action button are separate pressables. The whole field presses (opens the composer); action buttons are 56 by 48 pt and dim while held. A selection tick on pressing an inactive tab, on each tab the finger slides onto, on the field and on the minimized circle. While minimized, the bar box lets touches through wherever the design draws nothing (around the circle, and where the reply row was): `TabBarRoot` now makes a minimized floating box `box-none`. At full size the box keeps its touches, since a design may paint the box itself (Section Sheet's fallback bar does).
-   **Accessibility.** The field is a button named by its placeholder; each action has its own label. Tabs are read as tabs with their badge, by `TabButton`. The placeholder stops growing at 1.3× and then shrinks to fit rather than cut off.
-   **Tab count.** Made for five; three to six work. The lens fills a tab's cell but is never taller than it is wide, so with six or more tabs it stays a circle.
-   **Badges.** A count or a dot at the glyph's top right, in X's blue, with tabular figures.

## Demo

The example app gives Reply Stack its own screen per tab (`apps/example/src/reply-stack-screens.tsx`), not the shared photo feed: a post's page with its replies on Home (where the reply row belongs), trends on Search, a topic grid on Explore, notifications on Alerts and a message list on Chat. Invented people and words; X's dark and light colours.

## Out of scope

-   A text input in the row: it opens your composer, as X's does.

## Verification

-   The registry's bar tests cover it (one tab per route, presses through, works under a provider), plus five of its own: the compose button presses through and gives way to the reply row; the row presses through and each action is a labelled button; it is absent when hidden or when no `reply` is given; scrolling minimizes it to the circle, the tabs are then hidden and take no presses while the docked row still works, and the circle restores the tabs without changing tab; and with `minimizeOnScroll={false}` it stays full. The Jest stand-in for Reanimated now runs `useAnimatedReaction`, so these drive the real minimized state. A foundation test checks that a minimized floating box lets touches through and takes them again when restored.
-   An independent review (code, the clip, tests and docs) on 2026-11-27 found the press-only lens, the 58 pt circle and the shrinking icon, the placeholder timing, the blocked touches, the web's hidden tabs and a stuck lens after a quick second press; all fixed here.
-   On the iOS simulator (3x), compared side by side with the reference at the same scale: dark and light glass, the edge, the lens, sizes and spacing.
-   Frame by frame at 30 fps: the lens glide (about 130 ms across two tabs), the crossfade, the row's exit and entrance with the screen staying blurred under it, and with Reduce Motion on, every change in one frame.
-   The largest accessibility text size: the placeholder fits. VoiceOver labels from the accessibility tree.
-   Three and six tabs on the simulator: the lens is a wide capsule with three and a circle with six.
-   The web preview in WebKit (Playwright), left to right and right to left (`&rtl=1`): the row and tabs mirror, the lens follows, no page errors. Headless WebKit doesn't draw `backdrop-filter`, so the web blur itself is checked only from the CSS it produces (`blur(12px)`). Minimized, both ways: the circle and the docked row sit right (measured from the DOM: the row's edge at 62 pt, the circle 52 by 52), and a wheel over where the row was scrolls the feed.
-   Minimize on the iOS simulator, frame by frame: dark and light glass, six tabs (the circle keeps the active tab, Chat, and the icons gather into it), the largest accessibility text ("Pos…" docked), Reduce Motion (minimize and restore each in one frame), and a slide from Alerts to Home (the lens follows, Alerts stays solid, the tab changes on lift). Native right to left wasn't run: the example app can't switch it.
-   `feel` check and `unslop-ui` count: nothing to fix.
-   Still to do on a device: 120 Hz on hardware, including the shrinking clips' per-frame layout; Android's fill (no Android emulator or phone was available); native right to left; and the web blur in a real browser. In Expo Go's development build the lens sometimes stalls for 2 or 3 frames when a tab change mounts a new screen; check a release build.
